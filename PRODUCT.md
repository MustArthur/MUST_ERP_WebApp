# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Factory floor workers, QC inspectors, warehouse operators, production supervisors, and plant managers at a Thai food and beverage manufacturing company. Users operate in a factory environment: standing at workstations, often wearing gloves, under fluorescent lighting. Many are not IT-savvy. The primary task on any given screen is completing a specific operational step (record a receipt, start a work order, enter QC results) quickly and moving on.

## Product Purpose

MUST ERP manages the end-to-end production workflow for a Thai food/beverage factory: raw material receiving → QC inspection → production work orders → inventory → finished goods → customer delivery. The system replaces paper-based and Excel-based tracking. Success means zero lost batches, zero skipped QC steps, and any worker being able to complete their task without asking a supervisor for help.

## Brand Personality

Reliable, clear, matter-of-fact. Not playful or corporate. The system should feel like a well-organized workshop — every tool in the right place, nothing decorative, nothing that demands attention for itself.

## Anti-references

- **SAP / old Thai ERP software**: Dense forms, tiny text, overwhelming navigation trees, dated visual language. The thing users hate.
- **Western SaaS startup dashboards**: Glassmorphism, gradient-heavy hero metrics, animated number counters, SaaS-cream palettes. Feels wrong for a factory floor.
- **Excel spreadsheet UIs**: Tabular-first, no visual hierarchy, everything the same weight.

## Design Principles

1. **Task first** — every screen should make the primary task immediately obvious. Navigation and chrome are secondary.
2. **Trust through consistency** — same button, same form, same badge behavior everywhere. Users build muscle memory; break it and they slow down.
3. **Earn each interaction** — no confirmation modals for low-risk reads; reserve modal disruption for destructive or irreversible actions only.
4. **Thai first** — labels, messages, and guidance are in Thai. English terms (QC, CCP, FG, WO) are acceptable as industry shorthand when universally understood at the target facility.
5. **Dense but legible** — ERP needs information density; legibility at a glance beats minimalism that hides data.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA minimum. Touch-friendly targets (44×44pt) for tablet/floor-terminal use. High contrast for factory lighting. No motion dependency.
